01 / NATIONAL SNAPSHOT

Potential is not the same as installed power.

We separate solar resource, technical potential and installed capacity so the project remains scientifically meaningful.

WESTERN INDIA

High sunlight + open land

Large dry land areas and strong irradiation make western India especially suitable for utility-scale solar.

URBAN INDIA

Rooftops become the resource

Where open land is scarce, distributed rooftop systems can generate electricity close to consumers.

DEPLOYMENT REALITY

Sunlight alone is not enough

Grid access, terrain, roads, land ownership, ecology and demand influence what can actually be built.

EDUCATIONAL FORMULA

Annual Energy ≈ PV Capacity × Peak-Sun-Hours × 365 × Performance Ratio

A performance ratio near 0.75–0.85 is used for a quick educational estimate. The calculator defaults to 0.80.

!

Important limitation

This website does not replace site-specific GIS analysis, irradiation time-series, shading studies, grid-interconnection studies, land-title checks or detailed techno-economic modelling.

PROJECT CONCLUSION

India's solar opportunity is huge.
But it is not uniform.

Western and several central and southern states offer strong utility-scale opportunity, while dense urban areas can gain more from rooftop and distributed solar. Turning theoretical potential into real electricity depends on land suitability, grids, technology, policy, finance and environmental responsibility.
